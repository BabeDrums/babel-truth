# Babel Product Principles

These are cross-app product principles. App-specific jobs, workflows, and features remain owned by the relevant app repo.

## 1. Product need earns the product

Do not invent apps to complete an elemental set, fill Babel World, create visual symmetry, or justify an ecosystem diagram.

A focused product with a real job is better than a complete-looking family of unnecessary apps.

## 2. Shared ecosystem, distinct products

Babel products may share identity, standards, semantics, and infrastructure where useful, but each product must remain understandable and useful on its own.

Do not make every app a skin of FIRE or a skin of Babel World.

## 3. Shared meaning only when meaning actually transfers

If two products genuinely mean the same thing, preserve semantic compatibility. If their jobs differ, do not force a false universal model merely for consistency.

Examples:
- provenance is not achievement
- selection is not completion
- app usage is not proof of skill
- visual interaction between metaphors is not evidence transfer

## 4. Evidence before interpretation

For learning/progression semantics, preserve what happened, where it happened, and with what context before reducing it to a conclusion.

Interpretations may evolve; historical evidence should remain inspectable.

## 5. Local identity, honest shared foundations

Shared foundations should reduce relearning and inconsistency without erasing the personality/job of each product.

Consistency is valuable when meaning transfers. Distinction is valuable when the product job differs.

## 6. Delete before styling

When a label, helper, page, container, or feature does not make the task easier to understand or complete, prefer removing it over styling around the confusion.

## 7. Fundamentals before signature polish

Stabilize:
- information architecture
- navigation
- task purpose
- spacing and layout rhythm
- interaction states
- accessibility
- data safety

before using personality, lore, glow, pixels, animation, or thematic metaphors to create delight.

## 8. Metaphor should clarify or delight, not translate the task

Thematic language is welcome when it is immediately understandable or lives in an expressive/progression layer.

Do not force ordinary production/product concepts into a metaphor that makes the user decode terminology.

For FIRE specifically, this means Fire language can enrich the progression artifact and delight layer without automatically renaming Map / Structure / Tracks / Skeleton / Draft / Bounce or future functional concepts.

## 9. One product logic across device sizes

Phone, tablet, desktop, or future surfaces may adapt presentation, density, and modal form, but they should not become different products unless the job truly changes.

## 10. Build the smallest believable whole

Before deep polish, prove a thin end-to-end path that actually delivers the product promise.

Do not perfect isolated screens while the full workflow still depends on hand-waving.

## 11. Real-world validation beats internal certainty

Automated tests, browser simulation, and design review are necessary but do not replace using the product in its real context.

Physical-device and real-project validation remain distinct gates where relevant.
